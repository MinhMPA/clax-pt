Technical research brief · feasibility-first

Fast Differentiable Matter Transfer on GPUs

A controlled first pilot comparing a GPU-native sparse ODE backend with a structure-aware pure-JAX backend for linear Einstein–Boltzmann perturbations.

01 / Central idea

Exploit physics-derived matrix structure, not just GPU batching

For each Fourier mode k, first-order cosmological perturbation evolution is a linear system at fixed background cosmology. Its Jacobian is organized into a compact metric–fluid core plus largely nearest-neighbor photon/neutrino multipole hierarchies. Generic dense-Jacobian, dense-LU time-stepping may waste most of this structure.

dyk/dx = A(k, x; θ) yk + s(k, x; θ),   x ≡ ln a

The source s permits affine formulations or approximation-specific terms; verify exact linearity and sparsity against the implementation, including tight-coupling and radiation-streaming branches. Do not presume a simple banded-plus-low-rank decomposition without measuring fill-in.

→

→
Tc, Tb, Tcb → Plin(k,z)values and stable Jacobians

Hypothesis: sparse structure, better GPU execution and reuse of an implicit factorization for physical state and parameter sensitivities can reduce the dominant perturbation cost. This is a measurement to establish, not an assumed speedup.

02 / Deliberately limited scope

In scope for the first pilot

  • Flat ΛCDM with massless neutrinos (retain relativistic massless-neutrino perturbations).
  • Clax's existing background, thermodynamics, gauge, adiabatic initial conditions, TCA/RSA and photon polarization treatment.
  • Exact same perturbation right-hand side in both pilots; initially no physical truncation beyond existing Clax approximations.
  • Linear density transfers Tc, Tb, Tcb; linear Pcb(k, z) at selected redshifts.
  • Forward and differentiable outputs; separately measure perturbation-only and full-pipeline costs.

Out of scope (initially)

  • CMB angular spectra, line-of-sight/harmonic integration and CMB lensing.
  • Massive-neutrino momentum hierarchies, non-standard cosmologies and non-linear corrections.
  • A new recombination solver or redesigned tight-coupling equations.
  • Neural surrogates, learned regressions, fitting formulae or a wholesale new Boltzmann formalism.
  • A production HMC sampler until value/gradient correctness and performance are demonstrated.

Normalization contract: fix synchronous-gauge conventions, k units (Mpc−1), primordial-curvature normalization, density-transfer conventions, and the precise definition of matter. With massless neutrinos, late-time clustering matter is CDM+baryons; adding massive neutrinos later requires reporting both Pcb and Pmm.

03 / Two competing pilot options

Change one numerical layer at a time

Option A · GPU-native

Clax RHS → modax

Question: Can a fused CUDA Rosenbrock solver exploit per-k adaptivity and sparse LU sufficiently well at realistic ensemble sizes?

  • Extract a minimal Clax perturbation RHS, state ordering, initial conditions and consistent coefficient interpolation.
  • Port the RHS to modax's Numba-CUDA-MLIR-compatible callable; provide a conservative, regime-union Jacobian sparsity mask.
  • Run Rodas5P with independent per-mode stepping; compare 32, 128, 256 and 1024 modes.
  • Demonstrate first numerical equivalence with frozen coefficient tables, then the full derivative path through cosmological parameters.

Main upside: GPU-oriented kernel, sparse factorization and continuous forward sensitivities already exist. Main risk: porting/compile constraints, interpolation tables and chain-rule sensitivity across the XLA/Numba boundary; limited occupancy for small mode counts.

Option B · Native JAX

Structure-aware Clax Rosenbrock

Question: How much of Clax's cost comes from constructing dense Jacobians, dense LU and shared batched step control, without leaving JAX?

  • Instrument Rodas5Batched: RHS evaluation, jacfwd for state/time, factorization/solves, accepted/rejected steps.
  • Assemble the analytic linear Jacobian, compare entries with automatic differentiation, and measure cost with the existing dense LU.
  • Test only if justified block/sparse/banded or Schur-complement solves and grouping modes by step difficulty.
  • Retain the established JAX differentiation path; check accuracy and gradient stability after every change.

Main upside: lowest integration risk and native AD. Main risk: JAX sparse kernels/structured solves may underperform optimized batched dense LU for small systems; shared step control can still dominate.

Comparison rule: identical physics, initial conditions, floating-point precision, output redshifts and numerical accuracy. A faster but less accurate solver is not a pilot success. Neither approach should borrow accuracy from an emulator or changed physical approximations.

04 / Measurement plan

Baseline first; performance claims last

TestMinimum evidence
BaselinePin Clax/modax/DISCO-EB versions and hardware; reproduce a Clax matter-table solve. Profile background, recombination, perturbations, post-processing, compilation and device synchronization separately.
MathematicsCheck that ∂f/∂y equals the candidate analytic Jacobian; build the union of nonzeros across times, modes, parameters and TCA/RSA regimes. Quantify sparse fill-in and pivot/conditioning hazards.
Physics accuracyValidate density transfers and Pcb against the same Clax equations, then high-accuracy CLASS. Proposed science goal: ≤0.2% relative error in P over the accepted k–z domain, away from transfer-function zeros.
GradientsVerify ∂P/∂θ for ωb, ωc and h against high-accuracy finite differences / existing JAX AD; sweep finite-difference step sizes. Use absolute or scaled residuals near zero crossings, not unbounded relative errors.
ThroughputTime synchronized, warmed/JIT-cached forward and value_and_grad runs; report median and spread. Include 32–1024 k-mode scaling, GPU memory, kernel compilation and step-count distributions.
RobustnessTest a fiducial model plus ≳30 parameter draws spanning a declared ΛCDM box; check low-k horizon scales, BAO oscillations and regime-switch derivatives.

Suggested first working grid: 128–256 logarithmically spaced modes across 10−4 ≤ k ≤ 1 Mpc−1; z = 0, 1, 2, 3. Treat ≤0.2% in P as a proposed acceptance target, not a claim about either current implementation. Time both perturbations alone and the full P pipeline; an ODE-only speedup can be erased by thermodynamics.

05 / Design gates

What must be resolved before a full implementation?

Scientific & numerical blockers

Does the Clax RHS remain linear in the chosen state through TCA/RSA? Are its interpolation and regime switches differentiable enough for inference? Is the sparse pattern fixed and complete? Does unpivoted GPU sparse LU remain stable across the parameter box? Can derivatives of the background and thermodynamics be correctly propagated through the external-kernel interface?

Pragmatic stop/go rule

Continue with the fastest option that matches the required physics and gradients and shows a reproducible ≳2× perturbation-stage acceleration under matched accuracy. Defer or stop a route that cannot preserve the derivative chain, fails robustness tests, or yields no significant gain at realistic k-grid sizes. Record end-to-end speed separately.

A forward P runtime of ~0.2–0.5 s and forward-plus-gradient of ~0.5–1 s are aspirational later milestones, not acceptance criteria for this pilot. Mathematical validity and profiling take priority over an advertised speed number.
